## 1. PREMISE — one paragraph

Thirty years ago the world caught its first ember: a gate, burning in the air
above a river crossing. The crossing became a camp, the camp became a wall, and
the wall became **Threshold** — the only city ever built *around* gates instead
of away from them, because here and only here the gates burn stable enough to
be entered on purpose. Hunters walk in, clear what waits, and carry out **Ash**
— the residue of the other side, and the coin the whole city runs on. You are a
young hunter with a talent the Assay Guild does not like to name: when
something falls to you, it does not always stay down. It rises, near-black and
silent, and walks at your heel. They call it **the Bind**. They tolerate it,
because the Breach is getting worse — and because lately, for the first time in
thirty years, gates have started opening *outside* the walls.

## 2. THE TRUTH UNDERNEATH (the twist — owner's eyes only)

The gates are not invasions. **They are vents.** The world on the other side is
an ember-forest burning down to its last light, and everything that comes
through a gate is not attacking — it is *fleeing*. What drives the monsters
through is the thing that keeps the far world burning: **THE RIFT ARCHON** —
not a monster, but the first hunter. The first person to ever walk into a gate,
who kept ascending — path after path, exactly the ladder the player climbs at
level 55 — until there was no person left, only a crown of authority holding
the far world's fire so it burns forever instead of going out.

The six named bosses — **the Warden, the Gravelord, the Frostcaller, Infernus,
the Weaver, and the Archon itself** — are its six **Regents**, each holding one
piece of its delegated authority. Every **sigil** a hunter takes from a Regent
is a fragment of the crown. The **Ascension** the Exchange sells as a
weapon-craft is, without a single smith knowing it, the re-forging of that
crown — piece by piece, sale by sale. By endgame the player has personally
reassembled most of a crown that should never have been whole again.

**The Bind is the far world testifying.** Bound shadows keep their silhouette
because they keep their *memory* — the player's talent is not necromancy, it is
being the only person the dead of the far world can still speak through. This
is why the story's whisper-voice (see The First Voice, §5) arrives through the
player's own shadows.

**The final choice (Act III climax):** seal the Breach — which ends the gates,
and with them the Ash economy that keeps Threshold alive — or take the crown
and become the new keeper of the fire, holding the vents open on your own
terms. (Mechanically: two epilogue states, both leaving anomaly gates + dailies
+ endgame ladder open; the choice recolors the city's dusk palette and the
title screen, and NG+ hooks hang off "the crown remembers".)

## 3. THREE ACTS — anchored to the six shipped bosses

### ACT I — "THE ASSAY" (ranks E–D, levels 1–19)
The trade, learned honestly. Threshold works: you take contracts, you clear
gates, the Exchange buys what you carry out. But the **wild gates in the
Verge** are new, and the Guild is quietly frightened — gates do not open
outside the ring; that is the one rule the city was built on.
- **The Warden** (E): its sigil carries a mark the Assay Hall has no record of.
- **The Gravelord** (D): the first shadow that speaks — one word ("*more*") —
  through the player's newest bound creature.
- **Act break:** the class oath at level 20. Assayer Veyra names your path at
  the desk — and, privately, asks you to keep the speaking shadow secret.

### ACT II — "THE VERGE BURNS" (ranks C–B, levels 20–39)
Why are the gates spreading? The Vergewalkers — frontier hunters the Guild
calls heretics — have a theory the player slowly proves: the gates behave like
*pressure*. Something on the far side is squeezing.
- **The Frostcaller** (C): first glimpse THROUGH a gate at the far world — an
  ember-forest under snowfall of ash.
- **Infernus** (B): the Regent that talks. It does not want to fight; it wants
  to know *"does the fire still hold?"* — and it dies relieved.
- **Act break:** the **SEALED STAIR** trial at level 40. The Guild's secret,
  told at last: Threshold is not built beside the gates. It is built **on**
  one — the first gate, sealed under the Assay Hall, and the stair down into
  it is where hunters prove a class. (This is the level-40 class trial the
  progression system already models — awardClassTier — finally given its
  door and its reason.)

### ACT III — "THE CROWN OF EMBERS" (ranks A–S, level 40 → endgame)
- **The Weaver** (A): reveals the Regents' purpose and the crown's shape; the
  player understands what the sigils in their pack actually are.
- **Ascension** (level 55): the player steps onto the same ladder the First
  Hunter climbed. The First Voice (§5) stops whispering and starts *answering*.
- **THE RIFT ARCHON** (S, the Reach): the confrontation — and the choice (§2).
- **Post-game:** anomaly gates are the crown's memories replaying; daily
  contracts are the Guild Ledger; the endgame ladder (Wave F) is framed as
  keeping the vents from tearing wider, whichever ending was chosen.

## 4. FACTIONS
- **The Assay Guild** — order, ranks, the ledger. Runs the Assay Hall. Knows
  more than it files. Veyra's faction.
- **The Emberwrights** — the Exchange, the forges, the ascension craft.
  Callun's faction. Unwitting crown-smiths.
- **The Vergewalkers** — frontier hunters, wild-gate scouts, heretics who
  respect the far world. Maren's faction. Right about everything, too early.
- **The Watch** — the Breach garrison. Brann's faction. Distrust Binders on
  principle; the principle loses.
- **The Bound** — the player's shadows. A silent faction with exactly one
  privilege: they are the only characters who have seen both worlds.

## 5. CHARACTERS (all speak through existing/planned surfaces)
- **Assayer Veyra** — the desk at the Assay Hall. Oath-giver (the shipped
  class-oath flow is her scene). Keeper of the Stair's secret. Dry, precise,
  loyal to the city over the Guild when it finally counts.
- **Master Callun** — the Exchange. Warm, loud, unknowingly re-forging a
  crown. His Act III scene — being told what his life's craft has been — is
  the story's emotional low point.
- **Watch-Captain Brann** — the Breach wall. Opens hostile to the Bind
  ("things that fall should stay down"); ends Act III standing shoulder to
  shoulder with the player's shadows at the Reach. The approval arc.
- **Maren** — the Vergewalker camped in the east meadow (the existing
  camp_hunters_east POI). Wild-gate tutor, pressure-theory heretic, the
  player's window into the far world.
- **The First Voice** — the whisper through the shadows. Act I: single words.
  Act II: sentences. Act III: revealed as the shed conscience of the Rift
  Archon — the part of the First Hunter that ascension cut away, which has
  been riding the Bind because the player is the first person since who could
  carry it. It is the story's narrator, and it is asking to be brought home.

## 7. TONE
Frontier-town warmth over a cold deep secret. Short lines, working-class
hunter argot ("assay it", "carry out", "the walk home"). The horror is quiet
and economic: the city's prosperity IS the far world's death, and everyone
suspects it a little. Humor lives at the Exchange. Grief lives in the Bind.
No speeches longer than three sentences — this is a phone game; every line
must survive being read mid-combat-cooldown.
